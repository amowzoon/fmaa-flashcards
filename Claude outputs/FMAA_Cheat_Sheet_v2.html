The ten rules that answer the most questions

1Order of the cycle: post → adjust → close → reverse. Reversing is optional and is the exact opposite of the adjusting entry.
2Interest paid and interest/dividends received are operating. Dividends paid and treasury stock are financing. Buying or selling long-lived assets is investing.
3Indirect method: add non-cash expenses and losses, subtract gains, subtract increases in operating assets, add increases in operating liabilities.
4Current = within one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer. A short-term debt with intent and ability to refinance is long-term.
5Rising prices: FIFO gives lowest COGS, highest income, highest ending inventory. LIFO the opposite.
6Revenue is recognized when control transfers, not when cash arrives. Cash before performance = liability (unearned revenue).
7Cash dividend is a liability at declaration. Small stock dividend (under 20–25%) reduces retained earnings at market value; large at par. Stock dividends and splits never change total equity.
8Change in estimate (life, salvage, collectability, depreciation method) = prospective. Change in principle or an error = adjust opening retained earnings, net of tax.
9Segregate authorization, recordkeeping, custody, reconciliation. Internal control gives reasonable, never absolute, assurance. Board is ultimately responsible; management maintains.
10Vertical common-size base: total assets (balance sheet) or net sales (income statement). Horizontal: % of the base year; growth = index − 100.

Accounting equation and element definitions

Assets (debit) = Liabilities (credit) + Equity (credit)

AssetProbable future economic benefit obtained or controlled as a result of past transactions
LiabilityProbable future sacrifice of economic benefits from a present obligation, from past transactions
EquityResidual interest in assets after liabilities: contributed capital plus retained earnings
Revenue / expenseInflows / outflows from the entity's ongoing major or central operations
Gain / lossEquity changes from peripheral or incidental transactions (a laptop maker selling its own machine)
Journal / ledgerJournal = book of original entry, in date order. Ledger = book of final entry. General ledger = every account.

Principles

Accrual basisRevenue when earned, expense when incurred, whatever the cash timing. GAAP and IFRS require it; cash basis is tolerated only for small firms. Cash received before delivery is a liability.
ConservatismUnder uncertainty, book expenses and liabilities early, revenues and assets only when assured. Expense the ad campaign now; accrue the probable lawsuit; wait for the contract revenue.
ConsistencySame policies period to period; change only for a good, permanent reason.
MatchingExpense in the same period as the revenue it produced: COGS when sold, depreciation over the life, warranty and bad-debt estimates in the year of sale.
Accrual vs deferralAccrual: earned or incurred, cash not yet moved. Deferral: cash moved, belongs to a later period.

A.2 Recording transactions: the accounting cycle

The ten steps, in order

1Identify and measure the transaction in money
2Journalize: date, debit(s), credit(s), equal amounts, description
3Post to ledger accounts (T-accounts)
4Unadjusted trial balance: proves debits = credits; not a financial statement; cannot catch a wrong-account error
5Adjust for accruals and deferrals
6Adjusted trial balance
7Statements: income statement → changes in equity → balance sheet → cash flows
8Close temporary accounts through Income Summary into Retained Earnings
9Post-closing trial balance: permanent accounts only
10Reverse (optional): first day of next period, exact opposite of an adjusting entry
Tested order: posting → adjusting → closing → reversing.

Double-entry rules

  • Every entry has at least one debit and one credit, and the totals are equal.
  • Assets, expenses, losses grow with debits. Liabilities, equity, revenues, gains grow with credits.
  • Contra account: opposite balance to the account it reduces (Accumulated Depreciation is a credit inside assets).
  • Adjunct account: same balance as the account it adds to (Premium on Notes Payable is a credit).

Permanent vs temporary

Permanent: every asset, liability and equity account, including contra-assets. Temporary: revenues, expenses, gains, losses, drawings or dividends, Income Summary.

A.3 Financial statements and cash flows

Balance sheet (point in time)

Helps assessOperating capability; capital structure, risk, creditworthiness; enterprise value; future cash flows; liquidity (time to turn into cash) and financial flexibility (ability to change amounts and timing of cash flows)
LimitationsHistorical cost, not fair value; estimates and judgments; omits things that cannot be measured (people); snapshot only
Asset orderCurrent (cash, securities, receivables, inventory, prepaids) → long-term investments → PP&E → intangibles → other assets
Equity partsCapital stock at par · additional paid-in capital · retained earnings (free and appropriated) · accumulated OCI · less treasury stock. Disclose shares authorized, issued, outstanding.
DisclosureParenthetical notes, footnotes, cross-references and contra items, supporting schedules. Supplemental: contingencies, accounting policies, contracts and covenants, post-balance-sheet events.

Statement of changes in equity

Beginning ± prior-period adjustment + net income + shares issued − dividends declared = ending. Net income comes in from the income statement; ending equity goes to the balance sheet.

Income statement (period)

Sales − discounts − returns = Net sales − Cost of sales = Gross profit − Selling expenses − G&A = Operating income + Other revenues and gains − Other expenses and losses = Income before tax − Income tax = Income from continuing operations ± Discontinued operations (net of tax) = Net income
Helps assessPast performance; future cash flows and their risk; sustainable earnings; management's efficient and profitable use of resources
LimitationsDepends on methods and estimates (quality of earnings); past only; leaves out items that cannot be measured reliably (some unrealized gains go to OCI; brand value)
Single vs multiple stepSingle: revenues minus expenses. Multiple: separates operating from non-operating and shows gross profit and operating income.

Statement of cash flows

PurposeCash receipts and payments in three groups plus the net change in cash. Shows liquidity, solvency (operating cash flow is the key), financial flexibility.
LimitationsLeaves out non-cash transactions; classification takes judgment; historical.
Built fromComparative balance sheets, the current income statement, selected transaction data.
MethodOnly the indirect method is tested: start with net income, adjust for non-cash items and working-capital changes. Direct method is rare because firms do not keep cash-receipt and cash-payment records by category.
Worked bridge: Net income 40,000 + depreciation 18,000 − gain on machine 4,000 + decrease in A/R 5,000 − increase in inventory 10,000 − increase in prepaids 3,000 − decrease in A/P 8,000 = cash from operations 38,000. Investing: +18,000 sale − 60,000 purchase = −42,000. Financing: +41,000 stock − 20,000 loan − 10,000 dividends = +11,000. Cash 20,000 → 27,000.
Hidden numbers: depreciation expense = change in accumulated depreciation + accumulated depreciation removed on a disposal (cost − book value). Dividends paid = net income − change in retained earnings.
Interest paid is operating even though the loan is financing. Dividends received are operating; dividends paid are financing. Buying treasury stock is financing.

A.4 Internal controls

Governance and control

GovernanceThe system by which an organization is directed and controlled. Goal: long-term shareholder value while balancing other stakeholders. Risk management and control are components of it.
Agency problemOwners (principals) hire managers (agents) whose interests differ; the board monitors management.
ResponsibilityBoard: ultimate responsibility. Senior management: establishes and maintains the system.
COSO definitionA process, effected by board, management and personnel, giving reasonable assurance of: (1) effective and efficient operations, (2) reliable financial reporting, (3) compliance with laws.
Reasonable assuranceNever absolute. Collusion, management override and cost-benefit limits mean controls can fail. Cost must not exceed benefit.
Ultimate objectiveOrganizational effectiveness: achieving objectives and goals.

Risk vocabulary

ResponsesAccept · Treat (reduce, usually with controls) · Transfer · Avoid

Structure and management style

  • Centralized: standardized, easy to enforce. Decentralized: varied, hard to make uniform.
  • Rigid hierarchy (army): least flexible, least risky, most control. Flat and free: flexible, risky.
  • Commitment shows in: money for information systems, use of budgets and standards, attitude to auditors, open-door contact with staff. Warning signs: a small clique decides everything, aggressive reporting, pressure to hit earnings targets.

Segregation of duties: four jobs, four people

AuthorizationRecordkeepingCustodyReconciliation

  • Purchase-order issuer ≠ receiving recorder (stops fictitious-vendor fraud).
  • IT: separate requesting, authorizing, developing, reviewing, implementing a change.
  • Segregation does not stop collusion. When separation is impossible, use compensating controls: audit trails, application reconciliation, exception reports reviewed by a supervisor, transaction logs, supervisory review.

A.5 Managing daily finances

Working capital

Net working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities

Goal: shorten the operating cycle (buy → hold → sell → collect) and the cash cycle (operating cycle minus days you take to pay suppliers). More liquid assets earn less; shorter-term debt costs less.

PolicyFinancingResult
AggressiveShort-term liabilities fund longer-term assetsHighest return, risk of insolvency
ConservativeLong-term money funds even current assetsSafest, lowest return
Maturity matchingMatch asset life to financing termMiddle ground; a machine is financed with retained earnings or a long-term loan

Cash

Why hold itTransaction, precautionary, speculative. Profit is not a motive; cash earns the least.
How muchDepends on revenue pattern and seasonality, operating expenses, contingencies, growth opportunities, market conditions.
Forecast saysDeficit → arrange short-term financing early. Surplus → invest part of it.

Receivables

Credit policySet by top management, run by the credit department: credit period, credit standards, limits, discount rate and period. Balance extra sales against default (credit) risk.
Loosen itSales up, receivables up, bad debts and collection costs up, opportunity cost up. A cash discount speeds collection.
FactoringSelling receivables below face value. With recourse: you still cover defaults; a sale only if you give up control, the recourse obligation can be estimated, and the factor cannot force a repurchase; otherwise it is a loan. Without recourse: factor bears the risk, costs more, treated as a sale with a loss.

Inventory

TypesRaw materials (traceable) · work in process · finished goods · materials and supplies (not traceable)
Why hold itSupply and demand mismatch, production delays, quantity discounts, level operations, avoid stockouts
Two questionsWhen to order? How much?
Ordering costsCarrying costsStockout costs
Preparing and placing orders; discounts lost by ordering too littleStorage, freight-in and insurance on shipment, facility rent and depreciation, insurance, property tax, security, spoilage, theft, obsolescence, opportunity cost of the moneyLost sales and goodwill. Watch lead time: order placed → goods ready to use
Shipping goods out to customers is not a carrying cost.
Just-in-timeInventory arrives as needed; demand driven; no warehousing. Cuts carrying cost, theft, control effort, property tax; needs reliable suppliers (small safety stock); exposes waste and defective parts. Less carrying cost, more stockout cost.
MRPComputer system that turns the master production schedule into purchase and production schedules; tracks stock, orders, lead times, usage; fewer rush orders.

Payables

Trade credit is borrowing; use it only when cheaper than other money (cost depends on the discount you give up). Ten-step cycle: requisition → purchase order (legally binding) → receive and check goods (receiving report) → vendor invoice → three-way match → approval → record liability → authorize payment → pay → reconcile and confirm. Benefits: trade credit, no late fees, tracking, fraud prevention (duplicate invoices), smoother cash flow, supplier goodwill, better reporting.

B.1 Receivables, inventory, PP&E, intangibles, liabilities

Receivables

ValuationShort-term at net realizable value; noncurrent at present value. Recorded net of trade discounts. Notes receivable are written promises. Nontrade receivables (employee advances, dividends receivable) shown separately.

Inventory

PeriodicPurchases account all year; count at year end; COGS = Beg + Purchases − Returns − End.
PerpetualRecords updated with every purchase and sale; annual count checks them.
FIFOOldest costs to COGS, newest stay. Rising prices: lowest COGS, highest income and ending inventory. Same result periodic or perpetual.
LIFONewest costs to COGS, oldest stay. Rising prices: highest COGS, lowest income. Periodic and perpetual can differ.
Parts Co.: 2,000 @5.00, buy 1,500 @5.40, sell 700, buy 400 @5.65, sell 1,300. FIFO: COGS 10,000, ending 10,360. LIFO: COGS 10,860, ending 9,500.

Property, plant and equipment

Cost includesPurchase price, property taxes and import duties, transport in, installation and trial runs, interest during construction of a self-built asset (expensed once it is ready).
DepreciationSystematic, rational allocation of cost over the periods that benefit. Land is not depreciated. See the formula sheet.
Accelerated vs straight-lineEarly years: more expense, lower income, more accumulated depreciation, lower retained earnings. Gross fixed assets identical. Double-declining ignores salvage until the floor (final year 29,600 lands on 100,000).
RetirementSale, exchange, abandonment, involuntary conversion. Depreciate up to the date; remove cost and accumulated depreciation; proceeds − book value = gain or loss, shown below operating income within continuing operations.

Intangibles

PurchasedAt cost including costs to prepare for use; if exchanged for other assets, at the more reliable fair value.
Internally createdOnly direct costs such as legal fees. R&D is always expensed. Internally generated goodwill is never recorded; goodwill arises only when buying another company.
Finite lifeAmortize cost minus residual over the useful life.
Indefinite lifeNo amortization; impairment test at least yearly.

Liabilities and financing

Leave out of currentA debt due within a year stays long-term if it will be settled from non-current assets (a dedicated fund) or the company has both the intent and the ability to refinance (actual refinancing before issuance, or a non-cancelable agreement).
Short-term sourcesTrade credit · unsecured bank loans (promissory note; line of credit = available if the bank has funds; revolving credit = guaranteed) · commercial paper · secured loans on receivables or inventory (field warehousing, warehouse receipts), chattel mortgages, repurchase agreements · factoring · bankers' acceptances. A required compensating balance raises the effective interest rate.
Long-term sourcesTerm loans, leases, bonds (large reputable firms). Higher rates for the longer maturity.

Special cases

Right of returnRevenue only for units expected to stay sold; a refund liability (allowance for returns, contra-revenue); an asset for goods expected back. 40 units at 1,000, 4 expected back: net sales 36,000.
Repurchase agreementForward or call: repurchase price below original = lease; at or above = financing. Put option: no incentive to exercise = sale with return; incentive and price below original = lease; at or above original and above market = financing.
Bill-and-holdRevenue when control passes if: substantive reason, goods identified as the customer's, ready to ship, cannot be redirected.
Principal vs agentPrincipal controls the good before transfer: revenue gross. Agent only arranges: revenue = commission. Agent signs: someone else fulfills, no inventory risk, no pricing power, paid by commission, no credit risk.
ConsignmentConsignor keeps title and the inventory (plus freight); revenue only when the dealer reports a sale. Dealer records commission revenue and a payable. COGS on 10,000 of 30,000 cost with 1,500 freight = 10,500.

Equity

Common stockVotes, residual claim, pre-emptive right to keep ownership share. Par, no-par, book value, market value.
Preferred stockDividend preference and priority after debt; usually no vote; dividend not owed until declared; may be convertible, callable, sinking fund, participating. Cumulative: arrears (not a liability) must be paid before common. Noncumulative: current year only.
Treasury stockIssued but not outstanding: no vote, no dividend. Contra-equity; reduces assets and equity. Bought to lift EPS and ROE, support the price, or supply shares for compensation.
Retained earningsFree, or appropriated (restricted) by the board. Accumulated OCI holds gains and losses that bypass net income and retained earnings.
Prior-period adjustmentCorrection of an earlier error (cash to accrual, missed expense): opening retained earnings, net of tax; not in this year's income.
Change in principleAverage cost to FIFO, completed-contract to percentage-of-completion: cumulative effect, net of tax, to opening retained earnings.
Change in estimateUseful life, salvage, collectability, and depreciation method: prospective only.

B.2 Common-size analysis · B.3 Ratios

Vertical common-size

Each item as a % of the same year's base: total assets on the balance sheet, net sales on the income statement. Compares companies of different sizes, periods, or the industry average. "Advertising is 2% of sales."

Horizontal common-size (base year)

Each item as a % of its base-year value. Also called trend or variation analysis. Growth rate = index − 100. If sales go from 100 to 125, growth is 25%.

Largest change test: divide current by prior for every line. 500 ÷ 600 = 83% is a 17% move and beats +10%, +8%, +5%.

How to read each ratio family

LiquidityCan it pay what is due soon? Quick ratio is stricter than current because inventory (a library, a department store) may take long to sell. Too high a current ratio means idle assets.
LeverageHow much is borrowed and can it service the interest? Creditors like low debt ratios; cyclical firms keep them low; owners accept more because debt magnifies returns. Interest is tax deductible, dividends are not. Operating leverage = fixed costs magnify the effect of sales swings on operating income.
ActivityHow fast do assets turn into sales and cash? Shorter operating and cash cycles are better. Too low a DSO can mean credit terms so tight that sales are lost.
ProfitabilityMargins per dollar of sales; ROA is earning power of the assets; ROE is return to owners. ROE above ROA means borrowed money earned more than it cost. If operating margin falls while gross margin holds, the problem is other operating costs.
Fictitious inventory inflates total assets: total asset turnover falls unexpectedly. The current ratio would go up, not down. DSO and P/E are unaffected.
Average balances: (beginning + ending) ÷ 2. Beechwood's equity includes the reserve for bond retirement but not the bonds: (467,000 + 534,000) ÷ 2 = 500,500; ROE = 96,000 ÷ 500,500 = 19.2%.
From a quick ratio to inventory: current assets = NWC + current liabilities; inventory = current assets − quick ratio × current liabilities.
